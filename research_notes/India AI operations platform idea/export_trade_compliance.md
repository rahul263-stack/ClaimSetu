## 1. Scale: exporters, importers, filings, trade value, e-commerce exports

### Takeaway
India's merchandise trade is about US$1.2 trillion a year (exports US$441.8 bn, imports US$775.0 bn in FY2025-26), with MSMEs credited with roughly 48% of exports but only about 1.73 lakh MSMEs actively exporting. I could not find an official count of active IEC holders or of shipping bills / bills of entry filed per year; the available proxies are old or unofficial.

### Inferences
- If MSMEs are about 48% of US$441.8 bn merchandise exports, MSME-linked exports are roughly US$210 bn a year (consistent with ₹9.52 lakh crore in H1 FY26). Spread over about 1.73 lakh active MSME exporters this implies an average of roughly US$1.2 mn of exports per MSME exporter per year; the distribution is certainly skewed, so the median is much lower.
- The serviceable base for an MSME-focused operator is on the order of 1.5–2 lakh active exporting firms, not the "12.5 lakh" IEC figure, most of which appear dormant or import-only.
- The 2016 figure of 17,000 bills of entry a day would be about 6 million a year; this is my own multiplication of a ten-year-old figure and should not be used as a current number.

### Gaps
- No official count of active IEC holders (exporters vs importers) for 2025-26 found. The Commerce Annual Report 2025-26 ([PDF](https://www.commerce.gov.in/ministryofcommerce/sites/default/files/2026-04/Annual_0.pdf)) and DGFT data were not opened and may contain it.
- No annual number of shipping bills or bills of entry filed for any recent year found, despite four differently worded searches. CBIC annual reports, NTRS 2025 full report and Parliament answers are the likely sources.
- No reliable current (2025-26) value of e-commerce exports found.

## 2. Workflow and pain: documents, errors, cost/time, incentives lost, penalties, FEMA/RBI, FTA utilisation, CBAM/EUDR, US tariffs

### Takeaway
The workflow is a chain of linked filings (shipping bill → GST return match → e-BRC/EDPMS closure → incentive scrip), and an error at the shipping-bill stage is the usual reason incentives and refunds are lost, but nobody publishes a rupee figure for unclaimed incentives. The rule set moved a lot in 2025-26 (new FEMA regime from 1 Oct 2026, RoDTEP cuts and short extensions, CBAM payment phase, US tariff swings), which raises the value of a service that tracks rules continuously.

Classification errors and penalties
- Under self-assessment the importer or exporter is responsible for correct classification, duty rate, value and exemption claims; customs has pursued exporters who shipped the same goods under two different 8-digit HS codes. — [taxtmi case note](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=867802)
- 2025 CESTAT rulings set aside Section 114AA penalties where the exporter's classification merely differed from a later DRI view; one bench held 114AA is for paper-only "dummy" exports. Outcomes are fact-dependent. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/exporters-declaration-in-shipping-bills-not-false-merely-due-to-dris-alternate-classification-view-cestat-sets-aside-penalty-us-114aa-1432535); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/tax-cases/section-114-aa-customs-act-applicable-only-for-dummy-exports-made-only-on-paper-not-actual-export-of-goods-cestat-298833)
- Misdeclaration penalties under Section 112 can extend to the value of the goods; show-cause notices typically need a reply within 30 days (CHA marketing page). — [StartupGrants India](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/services/customs-clearance-cha-services/fees)
- From 1 November 2025 importers, exporters and customs brokers can voluntarily revise customs entries after clearance (Notification 70/2025-Cus (N.T.), Section 18A); fee ₹1,000 per electronic application. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/cbic-voluntary-correction-customs-entries-rules-fee-electronic-applications-125103101241_1.html); [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/cbic-notifies-voluntary-post-clearance-customs-revision-regulations.html)

FEMA / RBI (realisation, EDPMS/IDPMS)
- New regime effective 1 October 2026: FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 notified 13 January 2026, Directions issued 16 January 2026, amended by notification FEMA 23(R)/(1)/2026-RB dated 22 September 2026. It replaces the 2015 regulations, the Master Directions and 167 circulars. — [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/rbi-trade-regulations-2026-key-reforms-in-payment-timelines-realisation-reporting)
- Realisation period under the 2026 regime as amended: goods 9 months from shipment; services/software 9 months from invoice; overseas-warehouse exports 9 months from sale; INR-invoiced or INR-settled exports 12 months (cut from 18); project exports per contract. AD banks may extend under their own policies. — [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/rbi-trade-regulations-2026-key-reforms-in-payment-timelines-realisation-reporting)
- Conflict: the November 2025 amendment (FEMA 23(R)/(7)/2025-RB, 13 Nov 2025) had extended realisation from 9 to 15 months, and several sources still describe 15 months (18 for INR) as the 2026 rule. The L&S note says the September 2026 notification brought it back to 9 months from 1 October 2026. — [Taxmann on 15 months](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-extends-the-time-period-for-realisation-of-full-export-from-9-to-15-months); [EY alert](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2025/12/rbi-introduces-comprehensive-relaxations-for-exporters-and-importers); contradicted by [L&S](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/rbi-trade-regulations-2026-key-reforms-in-payment-timelines-realisation-reporting)
- Import payments under the 2026 regime follow the contract (previously 6 months, extendable to 3 years); services imports now expressly covered. — [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/rbi-trade-regulations-2026-key-reforms-in-payment-timelines-realisation-reporting)
- Small-value relief (RBI A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 12, 1 October 2025): EDPMS/IDPMS entries up to ₹10 lakh can be closed on the exporter's/importer's declaration, including value reductions, with declarations allowed on a consolidated quarterly basis. — [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/rbi-trade-regulations-2026-key-reforms-in-payment-timelines-realisation-reporting)
- Services EDF due within 30 days after the month of invoice; one consolidated monthly EDF allowed. Exporters on the caution list at 30 September 2026 stay bound until removed. — [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/rbi-trade-regulations-2026-key-reforms-in-payment-timelines-realisation-reporting)
- Bank-side cost example: SBI forex tariff (2018, old) lists ₹1,000 per shipping bill for an AD transfer request under EDPMS. — [SBI forex service charges](https://sbi.co.in/documents/16012/76239/Forex-service-charges_02072018.pdf)

### Inferences
- The incentive pool an operator could help exporters capture is large in aggregate (RoDTEP alone ₹10,000–18,000 crore a year, plus drawback, RoSCTL, IGST refunds and interest subvention), but the share that goes unclaimed is unmeasured; a "% of recovered incentives" pitch would have to be proven customer by customer.
- Because RoDTEP and drawback eligibility are fixed at shipping-bill filing, the operator has to sit before or at the filing step (pre-filing checks on HS code, scheme declaration, invoice/GSTR-1 match), not only do post-facto recovery.
- The ₹10 lakh declaration-based EDPMS/IDPMS closure and consolidated quarterly declarations reduce bank-side friction for small shipments, which lowers the pain (and willingness to pay) for that slice but makes it easier to automate.
- Rule churn in 12 months (RoDTEP halved then restored; realisation period 9 → 15 → 9 months; US tariff 50% → 18%; CBAM payment phase) supports a "rules kept current for you" value proposition.

### Gaps
- No published estimate of total unclaimed or lapsed export incentives (RoDTEP, drawback, RoSCTL, IGST refunds) found; only anecdotal vendor claims.
- No data found on HS misclassification rates, shipping-bill amendment volumes, or the number/value of penalties on exporters per year.
- No per-shipment documentation cost or hours figure from an authoritative source (World Bank B-READY / LPI data was not retrieved).
- No sourced list of documents per shipment was retrieved in this run (the standard set — commercial invoice, packing list, shipping bill, bill of lading/airway bill, certificate of origin, LUT/GST documents, e-BRC — is common knowledge but is uncited here).
- Advance Authorisation / EPCG export-obligation defaults, RoSCTL budget and duty drawback annual outlay: not found.
- EUDR impact on Indian exporters: no sourced data found.
- Post-30 September 2026 RoDTEP status: only an unverified "extended to 31 Dec" headline.
- The exact RBI text for the post-1 October 2026 realisation period was not read; relied on one law-firm note.

## 3. Who does this today: customs brokers / CHAs, forwarders, DGFT consultants, CAs, in-house staff

### Takeaway
Customs filing is done by licensed customs brokers (licensed by Customs Commissioners under CBLR 2018 after a NACIN exam) or by the exporter/importer itself; fees are negotiated case by case, with quoted ranges of roughly ₹2,500–5,000 per export shipment and ₹8,000–25,000 per sea import shipment. I found no official count of licensed customs brokers.

### Cited Findings
- CBLR 2018: Notification 41/2018-Customs (N.T.) dated 14 May 2018, amended 2019 and 2022. Licence granted by the Principal Commissioner/Commissioner of Customs; applicant must be an Indian citizen with Aadhaar and PAN, pass the exam (NACIN takes applications each August) and execute a bond. — [CBLR 2018 text](https://thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Regulations/Customs%20Brokers%20Licensing%20Regulations,%202018.pdf); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-obtain-a-customs-broker-licence-in-india-under-cblr-2018-eligibility-exam-and-process)
- Fee ranges (broker blogs and marketing pages; they do not agree): CHA fees ₹8,000–25,000 per shipment for sea cargo and ₹3,000–10,000 per AWB for air/express imports; another says ₹4,000–5,000 for a small or regular shipment; one agency lists export agency charges of ₹2,500–3,500 per 20' container and ₹4,000 for LCL. — [EximPe](https://eximpe.com/blog/b2b/freight-forwarder-vs-customs-house-agent); [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/custom-clearance); [Drip Capital blog](https://www.dripcapital.com/en-in/resources/blog/customs-house-agent-broker); [Kainaaz Associates](https://kainaazassociates.com/?p=372)
- One CHA provider publishes no rate card: fees "quoted on review of your specific case". — [StartupGrants India](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/services/customs-clearance-cha-services/fees)
- Other per-document charges exporters pay: Hapag-Lloyd India export documentation fee ₹5,300 per bill of lading from 1 January 2026, B/L printing ₹1,000; export service fee ₹1,599 per 20' and ₹2,177 per 40' at Nhava Sheva. Chennai CFS documentation ₹500 per container (2025-26). — [Hapag-Lloyd India local charges Q4 2026](https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/content/dam/website/downloads/pdf/India_Local_Charges_Export_Q4_2026_ERC.pdf); [Allcargo Terminals Chennai CFS tariff](https://www.allcargoterminals.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ATL-Chennai-CFS-Tariff-25-26.pdf)
- Directory counts (not licence counts): RevenueBase lists 610 "customs brokers" and 1,212 "customs brokers and clearing agents" companies in India (Sept 2026); GoodFirms lists 151. — [RevenueBase](https://revenuebase.ai/companies/customs-brokers/india); [GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/supply-chain-logistics-companies/customs-brokerage/india)
- Lending a customs broker licence to others is a recognised compliance problem under CBLR (legal commentary). — [Advocate Gandhi](https://advocategandhi.com/?p=40058)
- DGFT/EXIM consultancies (e.g. Afleo) sell scheme filing (RoDTEP, RoSCTL, MEIS/SEIS legacy) and scrip buy/sell services. — [Afleo](https://afleo.com/rodtep-scheme/)

### Inferences
- At ₹2,500–5,000 per export shipment for the customs leg, an AI operator cannot win on the filing fee alone; the revenue has to come from bundling the surrounding work (incentives, e-BRC/EDPMS closure, GST refund reconciliation, origin certificates, CBAM data) that brokers do poorly or not at all.
- Because an exporter may file its own shipping bills as an IEC holder, a startup has two legal routes: act as software/agent on the exporter's own ICEGATE credentials, or hold/partner with a customs broker licence. (Self-filing by IEC holders is implied by the ICEGATE API note in section 4; the statutory basis was not checked in this run.)

### Gaps
- No official number of licensed customs brokers (CBIC) or FFFAI membership found.
- No data on the split of work between brokers, forwarders, DGFT consultants, CAs and in-house export staff.
- No sourced fee data for DGFT consultants or CAs (e.g. % commission on scrips or refunds).

## 4. Government systems and integration: ICEGATE, DGFT, Trade Connect, ULIP, e-Sanchit, Bharat Trade Net, CBLR; who can file; APIs

### Takeaway
An API route into ICEGATE exists on paper: CBIC planned to accredit third-party software firms as "Customs Suvidha Providers" (CSPs) that file bills of entry and shipping bills for IEC holders and customs brokers, but as of the 2024 note there was no developer portal or sandbox, and I found no confirmation of live CSPs. Bharat Trade Net remains a budget announcement plus a draft bill, with no launch date.

### Cited Findings
- ICEGATE 2.0 API: Infosys was contracted to build an API interface that IEC and customs-broker applications can use to file bills of entry, shipping bills, IGM, EGM etc. Third-party developers are to be onboarded as Customs Suvidha Providers after accreditation by a technical committee (company documents, certifications, client testimonials; tax, data-security and ethics checks). — [taxguru note on API integration, 17 Jul 2024](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/note-api-based-integration-icegate-iecs-cbs.html)
- Draft CSP terms: one-year initial term, five-year total; annual ISO 27001 audit; non-exclusive, revocable right; 90-day termination without cause; CSP may extend API access to its own partner developers. Application is by email to DG Systems; no registration portal and no developer portal; sandbox "planned for the future"; testing in slots provided by ICEGATE. At the 5 April 2024 meeting all developers but one had tested the APIs. — [taxguru note](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/note-api-based-integration-icegate-iecs-cbs.html)
- ICEGATE added a payment-aggregator facility (UPI/cards) for customs duty in March 2026. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/cbic-introduces-payment-aggregator-facility-on-icegate-for-customs-duty-522370-2026-03-25)
- ICEGATE–Land Port Management System integration launched January 2026 (real-time two-way data exchange). — [Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/?p=266286)
- A DGFT trade notice of 5 August 2026 integrated voluntary duty-payment data from ICEGATE into the DGFT system for export-obligation discharge applications (search-snippet only; source link not confirmed). — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1066064)
- DGFT Trade Connect ePlatform is described as integrated with Bharat Aayat Niryat Lab Setu, letting exporters obtain digital authorisations and apply for incentives online (search-snippet only). — [taxtmi blog](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=877776)
- Bharat Trade Net (BTN): announced as a unified digital public infrastructure for trade documentation and finance; Union Budget 2026-27 announced its establishment; DGFT issued a draft Digital Trade Facilitation Bill on 9 February 2026 to give legal recognition to electronic trade documents; reported plan for a separate agency to run it. Described as digitising over 30 trade documents and linking ICEGATE, DGFT, GSTN, e-Sanchit, TReDS and ULIP by API. No pilot, launch date or onboarding numbers found. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/top-stories/union-budget-2026-2027-government-to-set-up-bharattradenet-as-unified-digital-platform-for-international-trade-521401); [Lexplosion on the draft bill](https://lexplosion.in/digital-trade-facilitation-bill-2026-key-highlights-you-should-know/); [Maritime Gateway](https://www.maritimegateway.com/plan-to-establish-a-distinct-organization-to-execute-bharattradenet-dgft); [Credlix explainer](https://www.credlix.com/blogs/bharat-trade-net-platform-guide-for-export-financing-and-documentation)
- IEC holders must update their IEC annually or face deactivation. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/reminder-for-importers-exporters-dgft-mandates-annual-iec-updation-to-avoid-deactivation-1446526)
- Under faceless assessment importers upload supporting documents online via ICEGATE (e-Sanchit) when filing the bill of entry. — [Customs Manual 2025](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Customs-Manual-2025.pdf)
- Electronic bills of entry and shipping bills became mandatory for personal-carriage goods from 1 May 2025 (Circular 09/2025-Customs). — [taxtmi](https://taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=14638&searchQuery=)

### Inferences
- The realistic near-term integration paths are (a) partnering with an incumbent filing-software vendor that already has ICEGATE connectivity or CSP status, (b) applying for CSP accreditation, which asks for client testimonials and past experience and so favours incumbents, or (c) operating on the client's own ICEGATE/DGFT logins with digital-signature authorisation. Path (c) is fastest but brittle.
- DGFT, EDPMS (bank-mediated) and GST portals have no startup-facing APIs evidenced in this research; much of the "submit and track" loop would run through portals or through banks and GST Suvidha Providers.
- If BTN ships with open APIs it could commoditise document exchange and help a neutral operator; timing is unknown and should not be assumed in a plan.

### Gaps
- Whether the CSP framework was finally approved, how many CSPs are live, and what they are charged: not found.
- ULIP API availability for trade-document use cases and e-Sanchit technical details: not researched in this run.
- Statutory text on who may file with customs (Customs Act s.146 / self-filing by IEC holder, G-card/F-card rules under CBLR) was not read directly.
- No public API documentation found for DGFT, Trade Connect or EDPMS.

## 6. Buyer willingness to pay and pricing models

### Takeaway
Hard evidence on willingness to pay is thin: the only observable price anchors are the per-shipment fees exporters already pay (about ₹2,500–5,000 broker agency fee per export shipment, ₹5,300 per B/L carrier documentation fee, ₹1,000 per shipping bill for some bank EDPMS services). I found no published success-fee (% of incentives recovered) rates.

### Cited Findings
- Export broker agency charges ₹2,500–3,500 per 20' container and ₹4,000 per LCL shipment (single provider's list); ₹4,000–5,000 per small/regular shipment in another source; ₹8,000–25,000 per sea shipment and ₹3,000–10,000 per AWB for imports. — [Kainaaz Associates](https://kainaazassociates.com/?p=372); [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/custom-clearance); [EximPe](https://eximpe.com/blog/b2b/freight-forwarder-vs-customs-house-agent)
- Carrier documentation fee ₹5,300 per B/L (Hapag-Lloyd, from 1 January 2026). — [Hapag-Lloyd](https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/content/dam/website/downloads/pdf/India_Local_Charges_Export_Q4_2026_ERC.pdf)
- Post-clearance correction costs ₹1,000 government fee per application, before any professional fee. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/cbic-voluntary-correction-customs-entries-rules-fee-electronic-applications-125103101241_1.html)
- CBAM reporting quoted at ₹15–20 lakh per MSME unit by industry (shows a much higher price point for new, unfamiliar compliance). — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/economy/eus-carbon-rules-enter-implementation-phase-putting-indian-msmes-under-pressure)
- Government money is being directed at MSME export compliance: Niryat Disha covers regulatory compliance support, and a 90% CBAM cost-cover scheme is reported as under design (possible subsidy channel for a provider). — [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-01-03/economy/two-credit-linked-sub-schemes-launched-under-export-promotion-mission); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/india-msme-compliance-costs-eu-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-2026/)
- Customs-broker draft CSP agreement shows ICEGATE intends to charge CSPs monthly (charges due within 7 days, 18% interest on late payment); amounts not visible. — [taxguru note](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/note-api-based-integration-icegate-iecs-cbs.html)

### Inferences
- Per-shipment pricing is capped by broker fees: an "all-in compliance" price of perhaps one to two times the broker fee per shipment is plausible only if it replaces the broker fee or demonstrably adds recovered incentives. This is my reasoning, not a sourced figure.
- Illustrative arithmetic only: RoDTEP rates are typically low single-digit percentages of FOB (rate range not sourced in this run), so on a shipment of ₹20 lakh FOB the incentive at 1–2% would be ₹20,000–40,000; a 10% success fee would be ₹2,000–4,000, the same order as a broker fee. A % model therefore works mainly for exporters with demonstrable leakage or large backlogs.
- New-regime work with high unit prices and no incumbent habit (CBAM data, rules-of-origin qualification for new FTAs, US tariff engineering) may carry better margins than routine shipping-bill filing.

### Gaps
- No survey or interview evidence on MSME exporters' willingness to pay for compliance software or managed services.
- No published success-fee rates for DGFT consultants (scrip commissions, refund recovery percentages).
- No unit-economics data (CAC, shipments per customer, gross margin) for any Indian trade-compliance provider.
- RoDTEP/drawback rate ranges were not sourced in this run; the arithmetic above uses assumed rates and must be checked.
