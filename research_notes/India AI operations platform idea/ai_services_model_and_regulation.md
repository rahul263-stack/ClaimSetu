# AI-enabled services / services-as-software model, and Indian regulatory and technical constraints (as of Nov 2026)

Reading note: "(search summary)" means the figure came from a search-result summary of the linked page and was not read on the page itself; treat as needing a spot check. "Unverified" means single low-authority source or conflicting sources.

## 1. Thesis and evidence: what investors say, market sizes, and critiques

### Takeaway
By 2026 the "sell the work, not the tool" thesis is consensus among top funds (Sequoia, Foundation, General Catalyst, YC, Emergence, Bessemer), justified by services/labour budgets being 6x-20x software budgets; the main critiques are structurally lower gross margins, heavy implementation and human-in-the-loop cost, unproven roll-up performance, and unresolved liability/insurance.

### Cited Findings
**Sequoia (Julien Bek, "Services: The New Software", dated 5 Mar 2026 per secondary coverage)**
- "For every dollar spent on software, six are spent on services"; the next $1T company "will be a software company that looks like a services firm." — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)
- Illustration: a company pays ~$10K/yr for QuickBooks but ~$120K for an accountant to close the books. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)
- Copilot (sells tool to the professional, who stays liable; e.g. Harvey, Rogo) vs autopilot (sells the outcome to the end buyer; e.g. Crosby for NDAs, WithCoverage for insurance). "Intelligence" work (rule-based) is automatable now; "judgement" work stays human for longer. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)
- Wedge advice: start where work is already outsourced and intelligence-heavy, because replacing an outsourcing contract is a vendor swap, whereas replacing headcount is a reorg. If you sell the tool you race the model; if you sell the work, model gains improve your margin. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)
- Sequoia labour-TAM map (US/global mix as stated): insurance brokerage $140-200B; accounting and audit $50-80B outsourced (US); healthcare revenue cycle $50-80B outsourced (US); claims adjusting $50-80B incl. TPAs; tax advisory $30-35B; legal transactional $20-25B; IT managed services $100B+; supply chain/procurement $200B+; recruitment/staffing $200B+; management consulting $300-400B. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)
- Other data points in the essay: US lost ~340,000 accountants in five years and 75% of CPAs are nearing retirement; ~70,000 ICD-10 codes in medical coding; tax advisory is 80-90% "intelligence" work. — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)

**Foundation Capital ("service-as-software", $4.6T)**
- Market framing: $4.6T annual enterprise spend on salaries and outsourced services vs a ~$200B SaaS pool. — [Foundation Capital, lessons from year one](https://foundationcapital.com/the-4-6t-service-as-software-opportunity-lessons-from-year-one/)
- Component cited: IT services + business process services ~$2.3T (Gartner); Gartner IT services $1.5T in 2024 rising to $2.3T by 2028 (search summary). — [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/ai-service-as-software/)
- Lessons: "Integration is not a post-sale activity. It is the product surface"; forward-deployed engineers are strategic; pricing should move from seat → usage → workflow → outcome, but outcome pricing is hard to standardise because results depend on the customer's own processes. — [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/the-4-6t-service-as-software-opportunity-lessons-from-year-one/)
- Critique inside the same essay: token costs are "far higher than the penny-level costs of traditional software infrastructure"; PoCs cost "headcount hours, not product clicks"; usage-priced vendors get "whipsawed" as model prices fall; "margin surprises" in the move to outcome pricing; "vibe revenue" risk. Enterprise legal compliance buyers expect 99%+ accuracy; Harvey priced at about $1,000 per lawyer per year (as cited there). — [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/the-4-6t-service-as-software-opportunity-lessons-from-year-one/)

**General Catalyst ("creation strategy" / AI-enabled roll-ups)**
- US services industries generate $6T+ a year vs a $370B software market; legal industry cited at $1.05T. — [General Catalyst](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/the-future-of-services)
- Targets: move single-digit growers to 10-20% growth and double profit margins "often to 30-40%" ("new Rule of 60"). — [General Catalyst](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/the-future-of-services)
- Portfolio claims (company/investor-reported): Crescendo gross margins rise to 60-65%+ at acquired PartnerHero and it can automate 80%+ of customer interactions; Long Lake (HOA management) 18 acquisitions, 25-30% team productivity gain; Dwelly (UK lettings) 6 agencies acquired, doubled EBITDA margins where fully deployed; Eudia (legal) 300+ person delivery team via Johnson Hana; Titan (MSP) acquired RFA. — [General Catalyst](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/the-future-of-services)
- GC earmarked $1.5B of its fund for AI-enabled roll-ups (secondary source); a claim that Long Lake reached $100M EBITDA in under two years is unverified. — [Capital and Clarity Substack](https://capitalandclarity.substack.com/p/the-general-catalyst-behind-15-billion)
- Titan raised $74M led by GC; claims its tools can automate up to 38% of typical IT tasks (company claim, search summary). — [ChannelE2E](https://channele2e.com/brief/titan-raises-74m-acquires-rfa-to-build-ai-enabled-msp-platform)
- Other roll-up capital: Thrive Holdings has $1B+ to deploy; GC has co-created at least ten roll-up startups; Metropolis raised a $1.6B equity+debt round in fall 2025. — [Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/inside-the-vc-roll-up-craze-that)
- Crete Professionals Alliance (Thrive-backed accounting roll-up): revenue above $300M with 20 firms acquired and a plan to spend $500M+ on acquisitions over two years (Reuters, June 2025). — [Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/thrive-backed-accounting-firm-crete-130200467.html)

**Y Combinator**
- YC's Requests for Startups asked for "full-stack AI companies" (services firm at the front, AI at the back) in 2025; the Summer 2026 RFS asks for "AI-native service companies – sell the completed work, not the tool", naming insurance brokerage, accounting, tax, audit, compliance and healthcare administration (secondary summaries; primary page not read). — [VC Cafe on YC RFS Summer 2026](https://www.vccafe.com/requests-for-startups-summer-2026-edition/); [YC RFS](https://ycombinator.com/rfs)

**Bessemer (India-specific, 6 Aug 2026)**
- Thesis: vertically specialised AI-native services companies built in India and sold globally are best placed to take share from the $264B Indian IT services industry; incumbents' headcount-linked pricing is exposed. NIFTY IT was down 39% from its Dec 2024 peak as of May 2026; top-five Indian IT firms averaged 1.2% constant-currency growth in FY26; revenue per employee flat for about a decade. — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-rise-of-vertical-ai-native-services-in-india)
- Taxonomy: copilot (tool; Cursor), autopilot (outcome, no human in delivery loop; Kintsugi sales tax), platform-plus-delivery (Wonderful AI), AI-native services (outcome, delivered alongside professionals who take responsibility; Crosby). Margins "track the complexity of the work, not the delivery model". — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-rise-of-vertical-ai-native-services-in-india)
- Five levers to software-like gross margin: own the whole workflow; build a learning loop from human interventions; route routine work to small models and cache; reuse IP across deployments; small high-skill team. Workflows named as best bought from specialists: pharmacovigilance, medical coding and claims, AML/KYC, regulatory writing. — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-rise-of-vertical-ai-native-services-in-india)
- Frontier labs are entering delivery: OpenAI Deployment Company raised $4B+ from 19 investors led by TPG; Anthropic launched an enterprise services firm backed by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman Sachs. — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-rise-of-vertical-ai-native-services-in-india)

**Critiques**
- Emergence Capital (Lotti Siniscalco, 10 Jun 2026, "The AI Roll-Up Trap"): acquired revenue may not stick, legacy pricing undercuts the new model, old workflows preserve old habits; the best AI-native services firms are built organically ("system, then trust, then scale"). — [Emergence Substack](https://emcap.substack.com/p/the-ai-roll-up-trap)
- Newcomer: "It's still early to assess the performance of the roll-up investments as a category"; roll-ups may return PE-like 2-3x rather than VC-like 10x. — [Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/inside-the-vc-roll-up-craze-that)
- a16z's 2020 essay already warned that AI companies "can even look more like traditional services companies" in their economics (older source, 2020). — [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-new-business-of-ai-and-how-its-different-from-traditional-software/)
- If the underlying service is a ~10% gross margin business, an AI entrant is "stuck in a price war" (blog commentary on YC's thesis; low authority). — [Omnius](https://www.omnius.so/blog/agency-business-model)

### Inferences
- The different headline numbers ($4.6T Foundation, $6T+ GC US services, Sequoia's 6:1 ratio) are spending pools, not forecasts of capturable revenue; they should be used only to show order of magnitude.
- Sequoia's "start with outsourced, intelligence-heavy work" maps directly onto India: BPO/KPO contracts already buy outcomes, so the buyer has a budget line and a vendor-swap decision.
- Two camps exist on how to get there: build organically (Sequoia, Emergence, YC, Bessemer) vs buy-and-transform (General Catalyst, Thrive). Evidence for roll-up margin claims is almost entirely investor-reported.

### Gaps
- No a16z 2025-2026 essay specifically on AI-enabled services margins was located; only the 2020 and 2015 pieces surfaced.
- Primary text of YC's RFS was not read; wording comes from secondary summaries.
- No audited financials for any roll-up (Long Lake, Crete, Titan, Eudia). The Long Lake "$100M EBITDA" figure is unverified.

## 2. Case studies with numbers

### Takeaway
The best-documented outcome sellers (EvenUp, Crosby, Harper, Candid, Rillet/Basis) price per case/document/commission, keep visible in-house professional teams for review, and have raised at $0.4B-$2B+ valuations; of the five companies the brief asked to verify, Hulp is a consumer concierge and Adalat AI is a non-profit court-transcription tool, so neither is a B2B regulated-workflow outcome seller, while Hesper AI, Altair Health and Statecraft are very early with almost no verified financials.

### Inferences

- Hulp and Adalat AI are weak analogues for a B2B regulated-workflow company: one is consumer concierge, the other a grant-funded public-good tool. Their relevance is evidence that Indian "AI + human" delivery and government adoption of AI in courts are both real.

## 3. Economics: gross margins, human review, outcome pricing, processing cost

### Takeaway
Benchmarks put AI-native software at roughly 50-60% gross margin against 75-90% for SaaS, with AI-services advocates targeting 55-80% at maturity after starting at 35-45%; raw document-AI processing now costs fractions of a cent to a few cents per page, so human exception handling, not compute, is the cost line that decides unit economics.

### Cited Findings
- Bessemer benchmark: AI companies ~50-60% gross margin vs 80-90% for SaaS (search summary of secondary source). — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/why-ai-gross-margins-are-so-much-lower-than-saas-and-what-that-means-for-your-business/)
- ICONIQ 2026: AI product gross margins ~52-53% projected for 2026 (41-45% in 2024-2025 depending on citation; 59% projected 2027) vs 75-85% traditional SaaS; inference averages 23% of revenue at scaling-stage AI B2B companies (secondary citations conflict slightly). — [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/ai-gross-margin/); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-pulse/ai-pulse-2026-07-31-ai-native-saas-gross-margins-are-still-running-20-40-points)
- AI-native services targets from practitioner guides (low authority): 35-45% in the first six months ramping to 70-78% after two years; 55-75% at maturity; bookkeeping example 55-65% early. — [Kanopy Labs](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/how-to-build-an-ai-native-service-company)
- General Catalyst's stated outcome: Crescendo 60-65%+ gross margin at an acquired contact-centre business; target EBITDA margins of 30-40% for transformed services firms. — [General Catalyst](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/the-future-of-services)
- Volatility warning: one commentator reports Harvey's gross margin swinging from about +50% to about -50% during 2026 as usage on rented frontier models spiked (single blog source, unverified). — [Object Edge](https://www.objectedge.com/blog/ai-native-services-need-a-company-brain)
- Bessemer: removing humans does not guarantee margin (commoditised AI chat); margin follows work complexity; human interventions should feed a learning loop so human cost per deployment falls. — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-rise-of-vertical-ai-native-services-in-india)
- Outcome pricing in practice: Intercom Fin $0.99 per resolution; Zendesk about $1.50-$2.00 per resolved ticket; HubSpot Breeze $0.50 per resolved conversation; Sierra pure outcome-based and past $100M ARR in Nov 2025 (search summary, mostly vendor/secondary). — [The Pricing Conundrum](https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/outcome-based-pricing-in-practice); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/ai-agents-charge-per-resolution-now-the-per-seat-model-didnt-stand-a-chance/)
- Survey: 18.7% of enterprises use outcome-based pricing tied to agreed metrics vs 17.1% per-user (search summary; survey origin not confirmed). — [Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/best-outcome-priced-ai-support-regulated-2026)
- Document processing cost (2026 list prices via secondary guides): cloud OCR about $1.50 per 1,000 pages (Azure/Google/AWS); Google Layout Parser about $10 and form/custom extractors about $30 per 1,000 pages; Mistral OCR $1-$2 per 1,000 pages (another source says $4; conflicting). — [UD](https://ud.hk/en/blogs/insight/article/ai-document-processing-costs-2026-08-11); [Activepieces, Google Document AI](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/google-document-ai-pricing-cost-per-page-2026-guide); [Activepieces, Mistral OCR](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/mistral-ocr-pricing-2026-cost-per-1000-pages.md)
- LLM extraction: Gemini Flash 2.0 estimated at about $1.67 per 10,000 pages. — [Vellum](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/document-data-extraction-llms-vs-ocrs)

### Inferences
- Order-of-magnitude arithmetic from the figures above: a 50-page case file costs about $0.008 with a cheap vision LLM, about $0.075 with cloud OCR and about $1.50 with a custom extractor; multi-pass agentic reasoning on a frontier model would be higher but still far below one hour of Indian operations labour (see section 4). Human minutes per case therefore dominate cost of delivery.
- Illustrative sensitivity (my calculation, not sourced): at a Rs 3 lakh/yr operations salary (~Rs 150/hour fully worked), 10 minutes of human review per case costs ~Rs 25; at a CA-level Rs 13 lakh/yr (~Rs 650/hour), the same 10 minutes costs ~Rs 110. Exception rate x specialist minutes is the key variable in an India cost model.
- Indian delivery makes the human layer 5-10x cheaper than the US equivalents used by EvenUp or Altair, so an India-based AI-services firm can tolerate a higher review rate at the same gross margin; this is my inference, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- No reliable published figure for BPO gross margins (Genpact, WNS, EXL) was retrieved; the SaaS-vs-BPO comparison is unsupported here.
- No published human-review/exception rates for any AI-services company.
- No primary source read for Bessemer, ICONIQ or Battery margin reports; all margin benchmarks are via secondary citations.
- Current per-token list prices for frontier models were not sourced.

## 4. India context: industry size, wages, funding climate, willingness to pay

### Takeaway
India's tech services industry is $315B (FY26E) with BPM at $59B and 5.95M employees but near-stagnant growth, entry-level operations labour costs Rs 1.2-4 lakh a year against Rs 12-13 lakh for a campus-placed CA, and funding is up in dollars but down sharply in deal count, with domestic buyers still paying far less for software than Western buyers.

### Cited Findings

## 5. India regulation cutting across workflows

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified on 13/14 Nov 2025 with substantive duties binding from 13 May 2027 and penalties up to Rs 250 crore, there is no general data localisation or special "sensitive data" class, AI itself is governed by voluntary guidelines, and the binding constraints for an AI-operations vendor come from sector outsourcing rules (RBI, IRDAI) and from professional-practice statutes that force a licensed human or regulated entity to own the output.

### Cited Findings
**DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025**
- MeitY notified the Rules on 13 Nov 2025 (PIB dates it 14 Nov 2025) and established the Data Protection Board. Phase I (13 Nov 2025): Board, definitions, procedure. Phase II (13 Nov 2026): consent-manager registration. Phase III (13 May 2027): notice and consent, fiduciary obligations, children's data, data-principal rights, cross-border rules, and the Board's substantive penalty powers. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/); [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Notice must be standalone, itemise the personal data and the specified purpose, and link to withdrawal/rights/complaint mechanisms. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- Breach: notify Board and affected individuals without delay, detailed report to the Board within 72 hours; CERT-In's six-hour reporting and sectoral (RBI, IRDAI, SEBI) reporting apply in parallel. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- Security safeguards (illustrative minimum): encryption/masking/tokenisation, access control, logging and monitoring, backups, one-year log retention, and security terms in processor contracts. The data fiduciary remains responsible for processing done by its processors. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- All fiduciaries must retain personal data, traffic data and logs for at least one year. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- Significant Data Fiduciaries: annual DPIA and audit, due diligence on algorithmic software, and possible localisation of government-specified data sets. Cross-border transfer is otherwise permitted except to countries the government restricts by notification. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- Children's data: verifiable parental consent, with exemptions for clinical establishments and healthcare professionals for welfare functions. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)
- Penalties (Schedule to the Act): up to Rs 250 crore for failure to take reasonable security safeguards; up to Rs 200 crore for failure to notify a breach and for children's-data violations; up to Rs 50 crore residual (figures via PIB overview and secondary summaries; verify against the Gazette). — [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf); [DPDPA Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html); [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=869933)
- Health/financial data: DPDP has no separate "sensitive personal data" category. The older SPDI Rules 2011 (which do treat financial and health data as sensitive) remain in force until Section 43A of the IT Act is omitted on 13 May 2027; until then both regimes apply. — [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/what-happens-spdi-rules-after-dpdp-enforcement/)
- Consent managers: Indian company, minimum net worth Rs 2 crore, data-blind, seven-year consent records; modelled on the Account Aggregator framework (net-worth figure via search summary). — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)

**RBI outsourcing**
- Master Direction on Outsourcing of IT Services issued 10 Apr 2023, effective 1 Oct 2023, for banks, NBFCs, co-operative banks, credit information companies and AIFIs: board-approved outsourcing policy; outsourcing must not impede the regulated entity's oversight; service provider must report cyber incidents without undue delay so the entity can report to RBI within six hours; legacy agreements to comply by renewal or 9/10 Apr 2026 (search summary). — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12486); [Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2023/04/rbi-regulates-outsourcing-of-it-services-by-financial-entities/)
- In late Nov 2025 RBI issued entity-wise "Managing Risks in Outsourcing" Directions, 2025; law-firm summary says agreements must allow audits by the entity's internal and external auditors and direct RBI supervisory access to the service provider, and offshore providers must keep records accessible in India (secondary source; final text for commercial banks/NBFCs not verified). — [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/corporate/rbi-outsourcing-directions-2025/); [RBI, rural co-operative banks direction](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/d/rbi/rural-co-operative-banks-managing-risks-in-outsourcing)

**IRDAI outsourcing**
- The 2017 Outsourcing Regulations barred insurers from outsourcing decision-making in underwriting and claims, and policyholder grievance redressal. — [TaxGuru, IRDAI 2017 regulations](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/insurance-guidelines-on-outsourcing-of-activities-by-insurance-companies.html)
- A June 2025 law-firm note says the 2017 regulations were superseded by a newer Outsourcing Framework under which insurers may outsource any non-core activity; policy servicing and appointment of surveyors, formerly core, can now be outsourced (search summary; instrument name and current core list not verified). — [TT&A](https://tta.in/?p=12718)

**AI-specific governance**
- India AI Governance Guidelines released by MeitY on 5 Nov 2025: seven sutras (Trust is the Foundation; People First; Fairness and Equity; Innovation over Restraint; Accountability; Understandable by Design; Safety, Resilience and Sustainability), adapted from RBI's FREE-AI report; a separate AI law "is not needed at this stage"; gaps to be fixed by targeted amendments to existing law; institutions include an AI Governance Group, a Technology and Policy Expert Committee and the AI Safety Institute; guidelines are voluntary. — [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/new-technology/1705690/ai-governance-guidelines); [BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/meitys-new-ai-governance-guidelines-set-seven-sutras-and-a-six-pillar-framework-10626308); [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/indias-new-ai-governance-guidelines-innovation-with-guardrails/)
- IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Amendment Rules 2026, notified 10 Feb 2026 and in force 20 Feb 2026, require labelling of "synthetically generated information" by intermediaries; aimed at deepfakes rather than B2B document workflows. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/meity-notifies-it-rules-to-curb-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/story/govt-tightens-rules-on-ai-generated-content-mandates-labels-and-faster-takedowns-515505-2026-02-10)
- No standalone Indian statute governs AI liability as of mid-2026 (search summary). — [Treelife](https://treelife.in/legal/the-ai-indemnity-trap/)

**Professional-practice restrictions**
- Bar Council of India told Parliament that AI use in legal practice is not prohibited, that it has powers under the Advocates Act to set standards, and that AI use is no defence to professional misconduct (lawyers remain liable for hallucinated citations). — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/can-lawyers-face-action-for-ai-generated-fake-citations-heres-what-bci-told-parliament)
- A Bar Council of Maharashtra & Goa notice to a legal-advice platform reportedly said only enrolled advocates may practise and legal startups must employ enrolled advocates or stay purely informational (secondary site, cites Section 29 Advocates Act; low authority). — [SupremeToday](https://supremetoday.ai/bar-council-of-maharashtra-goa-warns-society-first-for-legal-advice-without-enrolment-20260903066)
- BCI also holds that foreign lawyers/firms cannot practise Indian law (search summary). — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/headlines/3530684-bar-council-of-india-flags-unlawful-tie-ups-between-indian-and-foreign-law-firms)

**E&O / professional indemnity**
- Global market is ending "silent AI" cover: insurers are adding AI exclusions ahead of 2026 renewals; some (e.g. Berkley) introduced absolute AI exclusions across D&O, E&O and fiduciary lines; ISO endorsement CG 40 47 (available Jan 2026) lets insurers exclude generative-AI injury from general liability (search summary). — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/end-silent-ai-emerging-ai-exclusions-coverage-fragmentation-and-practical-implications); [Risk Management Magazine](https://www.rmmagazine.com/articles/article/2026/08/04/protecting-your-organization-from-ai-insurance-exclusions)
- Affirmative AI cover exists: Armilla (Lloyd's-backed; Chaucer, AXIS, Convex), CFC embedded AI cover, Cowbell standalone AI-agent liability policy for SMEs via wholesale brokers. — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/cowbell-launches-standalone-ai-agent-liability-policy-for-smes-596799.aspx); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/big-insurance-backs-away-from-ai-risk-and-startups-rush-in/)
- India: insurers now require companies to disclose AI use when applying for cyber cover; Marsh India (May 2026) says firms are buying cyber insurance against "AI liability"; Tata AIG notes cyber, professional indemnity and D&O address different exposures and buyers should check whether E&O responds to algorithmic harm (search summaries). — [Tata AIG](https://www.tataaig.com/knowledge-center/cyber-security/ai-cyber-risks-business-insurance); [inkl/ET](https://www.inkl.com/news/agentic-anxiety-pushes-it-to-run-for-policy-cover)

### Inferences
- An AI-operations vendor serving Indian banks, insurers or hospitals will normally be a data processor; the client (data fiduciary) carries DPDP liability and will push it down by contract (security terms, audit rights, breach notice faster than 72 hours, RBI's six-hour chain). Contract readiness, not a licence, is the gating item.
- No general localisation rule applies to a non-SDF processor under DPDP, but RBI-regulated clients will require India-accessible records and regulator audit access, which in practice means India-region hosting and careful choice of LLM provider region.
- Because IRDAI (at least under the 2017 text) reserves claims and underwriting decisions to the insurer, and the Advocates Act reserves legal practice to advocates, the defensible product shape is "prepare and recommend, licensed human or regulated client decides". This mirrors Crosby/Harper becoming the regulated entity themselves.
- DPDP substantive obligations start 13 May 2027, roughly six months after this note; a company launching now should build consent/notice, erasure and logging to the 2027 standard from day one, while still meeting SPDI Rules for health and financial data in the interim.
- Insurance: a startup should seek tech E&O plus cyber with affirmative AI wording confirmed in writing, since generic policies increasingly exclude AI output errors.

- MeitY's March 2024 AI advisory was not retrieved.

- Final RBI 2025 outsourcing directions for commercial banks and NBFCs were not read; whether the 2023 IT-outsourcing direction is repealed per entity type is unconfirmed.
- No rupee premium or limit benchmarks for Indian tech E&O/PI cover; no Indian insurer product with affirmative AI cover identified.
- ABDM health-data-management policy and RBI payment-data localisation were not researched.

## 6. Public digital infrastructure a startup can build on

### Takeaway
Account Aggregator is the most mature rail (about 500-600 million fulfilled consents, 800+ data users, an RBI-recognised SRO since June 2026), Aadhaar authentication reopened to private entities in 2025, NHCX has insurers connected but slow hospital uptake, and Bima Sugam is only in a phased, motor-first rollout; access terms for GSTN, ULIP, ONDC and DigiLocker were not established in this research.

### Cited Findings
- Account Aggregator: Sahamati (2 Sep 2026) says 500M+ fulfilled consents and Rs 3.82 lakh crore of lending enabled in FY26; a third-party page citing Sahamati's dashboard gives 595.11M consents and 352.19M linked accounts as of 30 Sep 2026; 806 FIUs live; Sahamati recognised by RBI as the AA ecosystem's SRO in June 2026; daily consents above 7 lakh (search summaries; bases differ between sources). — [Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/?p=1274421); [ProductGrowth](https://productgrowth.in/numbers/account-aggregator-consents-india/); [Sahamati H1 FY26 report](https://sahamati.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Credit-Reimagined-H1-FY26-Abridged-1.pdf)

- Aadhaar: the Aadhaar Authentication for Good Governance (Social Welfare, Innovation, Knowledge) Amendment Rules, 2025, notified 31 Jan 2025, allow non-government entities to use Aadhaar authentication (examples: e-commerce, travel, hospitality, healthcare) on a voluntary basis, with applications routed through the relevant central or state ministry. — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leadership/Aadhaar-authentication-for-private-entities); [IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/india-opens-aadhaar-authentication-to-private-sector); [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202502/uidai-expands-aadhaar-authentication-to-private-sector-for-public-interest-services)

- DigiLocker is recognised in the DPDP Rules as a route to verify a parent's identity via a virtual token from a notified digital-locker provider. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)

### Inferences
- For financial-document workflows (lending, underwriting, reconciliation), AA gives consented, machine-readable bank/GST-linked data and reduces the document-AI burden; but only RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA-regulated entities can be FIUs, so a startup would typically act as a technology service provider to a regulated client (general knowledge of the AA framework; not sourced in this research).
