## 1. Tax: volumes, pain, who pays (GST registrants, returns, e-invoices, ITC mismatch, notices, appeals, GSTAT, TDS, CAs and fees)

### Takeaway
The GST base is very large and machine-readable (1.67 crore active registrants, about 21 crore e-invoices a month, 156 crore e-way bills a year), and the dispute layer is large and newly unblocked: about 1.8 lakh first appeals pending and 4 lakh+ first-appeal orders that became appealable when GSTAT opened in 2025-26. I could not find an official national count of GST notices (DRC-01 / ASMT-10) or an official size of the ITC mismatch; those are the two biggest holes in the tax fact base.

**ITC mismatch and reconciliation**
- DRC-01B (GSTR-1 vs 3B liability gap) and DRC-01C (ITC in GSTR-3B exceeding GSTR-2B, Rule 88D) are system-generated intimations; the GSTN report says they drove "substantial" self-compliance June 2025 to May 2026 but gives no count. **[fetched]** — [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/nine-years-of-gst-taxpayer-base-crosses-1-67-crore-as-indias-digital-indirect-tax-ecosystem-scales-new-milestones-report-highlights-expansion-of-taxpayer-base-192-27-crore-returns-filed-an/)
- Notice mechanics: ASMT-10 scrutiny notice (s.61, Rule 99), reply in ASMT-11 within 30 days; DRC-01 show-cause notice under s.73/74 (to FY 2023-24) or s.74A (FY 2024-25 onward), reply in DRC-06. **[snippet]** — [Taxmann on ASMT-10](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/handling-of-scrutiny-of-returns-asmt-10-under-gst); [TaxGuru on 2B vs 3B notices](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-notice-itc-mismatch-gstr-2b-gstr-3b-reply.html)
- Courts have held that a GSTR-2A/3B difference alone does not justify ITC denial (Kerala HC line of cases), which is why these notices are contestable and generate reply work. **[snippet]** — [Taxscan case digest](https://www.taxscan.in/gst-case-digest-on-rejection-of-itc-claims-based-on-gstr-2a-vs-gstr-3b-mismatch/404733)

### Inferences
- E-invoice run-rate: 21.4 crore IRNs in June 2026 implies roughly 250 crore (2.5 billion) e-invoices a year, from only about 8.6 lakh GSTINs. E-invoices being 67.8% of GSTR-1 invoice count implies roughly 370 crore B2B invoices a year reported in GSTR-1. This is my arithmetic on a single month, not a published annual figure.
- Annual returns: 1.49 crore normal taxpayers filing GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B (monthly or quarterly under QRMP) plus composition quarterly returns suggests on the order of 20-30 crore returns a year. Rough estimate; the report gives only the 192 crore cumulative figure.
- Who pays: revenue is extremely concentrated. Roughly 65% of GST cash comes from taxpayers above ₹100 crore turnover (under 1% of registrants). Notice value and litigation budgets will follow the same curve, so a notice/litigation service is a mid-market and enterprise product by value, while the 1.3 crore proprietorships are a low-ticket, CA-intermediated market (₹1,500-5,000 a month).
- The appeal wave is time-boxed: the 30 June 2026 deadline for backlog GSTAT appeals has passed, so the filing surge has already happened; the continuing work is hearings, rejoinders and document production on a stock that could be several lakh appeals, plus fresh s.74A notices for FY 2024-25 onward.
- If 1.8 lakh first appeals are pending and 4 lakh+ orders were appealable, and each needs a paper book, grounds, pre-deposit computation and hearing tracking, the addressable document-preparation events are in the lakhs per year before counting ASMT-10/DRC-01 replies, which are plausibly an order of magnitude more numerous (no official count).
- Income-tax disputes are larger in value than GST disputes on available numbers (₹16.75 lakh crore at CIT(A) alone).

## 2. Tax competitors, GSP/ASP regime and whether "notice response and litigation as a service" is a real gap

### Takeaway
Filing, e-invoicing and 2B reconciliation are crowded and commoditised (Clear, Zoho, Tally, IRIS, Cygnet, Masters India, Vyapar TaxOne/Suvit, 62 GSPs). Notice handling exists mostly as trackers and reply-drafting copilots sold to CAs or enterprise tax teams (Clear Notice Management, Litigation Shift, Trackase, TaxReplies, GST Reply AI at ₹299 per reply, ICAI-listed tools); I found no funded company that takes a notice through to filed reply, hearing and appeal as an outcome-priced service, though the evidence here is thin and the absence is not proven.

### Inferences
- Is notice response and litigation as a service a real gap? Partly. Supply exists at two ends: (1) CAs and tax advocates who do it bespoke, and (2) trackers and drafting copilots. What I did not find is a scaled operator that owns intake from the portal, evidence assembly from books and 2B, drafting, filing under authorisation, hearing calendar and appeal, priced per notice or on outcome. Clear's assisted notice plan is the closest incumbent offering and Clear has the distribution to extend it.
- The structural constraint is s.116: a company cannot itself appear. An AI operations company must either employ CAs/advocates who sign and appear, sell to the taxpayer's in-house "regular employee" team (enterprise), or sell to CA firms as the back office. Each implies a different margin structure.
- API access is not the moat for returns (any ASP can get it through one of 62 GSPs). Notices, replies (DRC-06, ASMT-11), appeals (APL-01) and GSTAT filing are portal-and-PDF workflows; I saw no evidence of official APIs for these, so end-to-end automation there likely depends on credentialed portal sessions with taxpayer authorisation and OTPs. That is an operational and compliance risk to verify.
- Price anchors are low at the bottom (₹299 per AI reply) and the CA channel is being consolidated by Vyapar/Suvit and Clear. A new entrant competing on drafting alone has no pricing power; value sits in high-demand notices where the fee can scale with disputed amount.

### Gaps
- Not found: funding, revenue, pricing or traction for Zoho (GST/Books), Tally, IRIS Business Services (GST and LMS product), Cygnet, Masters India, Optotax, EZTax. Two searches returned nothing usable; these should be treated as unresearched, not as weak.
- No evidence either way on whether GSTN exposes notice/reply/appeal APIs to GSPs; current GSP fee schedule and per-call pricing not found (eligibility figures are from 2017 sources).
- No data on market price for a notice reply or appeal by CAs/tax advocates (per-notice fee, percentage of demand).
- "Trackase" launch was mentioned in a search summary without a clear URL.

## 3. Legal: volumes, high-volume procedural workflows, e-filing, Bar Council constraints

### Takeaway
Volume is enormous and concentrated in a few repeatable case types: 5.2 crore district-court cases, 44.7 lakh cheque-bounce cases, 10.7-12.4 lakh motor-accident claims worth about ₹96,000 crore, 5.7 lakh consumer cases, 1.78 lakh DRT cases. ### Inferences
- The best-fit workflows for an end-to-end operator are those with an institutional payer, a template-driven filing and a document set the payer already holds: s.138 complaints and loan-recovery arbitration (lenders/NBFCs), SARFAESI/DRT applications (banks), MACT defence and settlement (22 general insurers facing about ₹96,000 crore of pending claims), and consumer-commission defence. These payers are corporates, so the Bar Council advertising and fee-sharing rules bite less: the company sells software plus paralegal operations to the client and its empanelled advocates sign and appear.
- An "AI law firm" in the US sense (Crosby-style) is not available in India under Rule 2: a company with non-advocate shareholders cannot take legal fees and share them with advocates. Workable structures are (a) technology and operations vendor to in-house teams, (b) vendor to law firms and panel advocates, (c) ODR institution administering arbitration under the Arbitration Act, which is how Presolv360 and peers operate.
- Court-side automation is uneven: e-filing 3.0 is not universal, 3,100 crore pages are targeted for digitisation and only about 20% are done, so physical filing, certified copies and in-person appearances remain an exception path that needs local advocates. That raises the exception rate compared with tax, where portals are national.
- 20 lakh advocates against 5.9 crore pending cases, with most advocates solo, means the realistic small-practice buyer has low willingness to pay; the institutional litigant is the buyer.

### Inferences
- Tax vs legal as the first workflow for an "AI operations company":
  - Tax is more automatable end to end: national portals, structured data (e-invoice, 2B, 26AS), defined forms and deadlines, non-lawyers (CAs, employees, GST practitioners) may represent, and no advertising bar on a company. Weaknesses: crowded reconciliation layer, low price anchors, strong incumbents with distribution (Clear, Vyapar/Suvit, Tally, Zoho), and no confirmed APIs for notices and appeals.
  - Legal has larger visible stock (crores of cases) and clear institutional payers, but higher exception rates (uneven e-filing, physical appearances), longer cycles (years), and a hard structural bar on a company practising law or sharing fees.
- Pricing power in both comes from tying fee to amount at stake (demand in a notice, claim value in a recovery) and from serving the top few percent of payers; per-document pricing at SME level converges on a few hundred rupees.
- Liability: because a licensed professional signs, an operator will likely need employed or empanelled CAs/advocates and professional-indemnity cover, and should expect clients to seek contractual indemnity for missed deadlines (appeal limitation of 3 months, ASMT-11 30 days). This is reasoning, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on exception rates (share of notices or cases needing human handling), error rates of AI-drafted replies, or outcomes (demand dropped/reduced).
